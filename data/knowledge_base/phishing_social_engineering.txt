TITLE: Phishing and Social Engineering Indicators

Phishing attempts may impersonate banks, government organizations, companies or service providers.

Common indicators include:

- Requests for passwords
- Requests for OTPs
- Requests for PINs
- Requests for account credentials
- Urgent instructions
- Threats that an account will be blocked
- Requests to click unexpected links
- Requests to make payments
- Claims of prizes or financial rewards
- Requests for identity information

Social engineering attempts may create fear, urgency, excitement or authority pressure.

A suspicious communication should not be trusted solely because it uses an organization name or official-looking language.

Important claims should be independently verified through a trusted official channel.

An unknown sender is not automatically fraudulent.

Lack of previous reports does not prove that a sender is safe.
